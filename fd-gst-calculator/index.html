How FD Interest is Calculated in India

Fixed Deposits (FDs) are one of the most popular investment vehicles in India due to their guaranteed returns and low-risk profile. When you open an FD with an Indian bank or financial institution, your interest calculation typically follows a quarterly compounding method. This means that every three months, the interest earned is added back to your principal amount, and for the next three months, you earn interest on this new, higher amount. This 'interest on interest' mechanism makes FD returns significantly better than simple interest over longer tenures.

The formula used for compounding FD interest is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where 'A' is the maturity amount, 'P' is the principal, 'r' is the annual interest rate, 'n' is the number of times interest compounds per year (usually 4 for quarterly), and 't' is the time in years. In India, Senior Citizens usually receive a 0.50% higher interest rate than the general public. It's also important to note that interest earned on FDs is taxable under 'Income from Other Sources' as per your income tax slab, and banks deduct TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) at 10% if the interest income exceeds ₹40,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for senior citizens).

Understanding GST Tax Slabs in India

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based tax that replaced various indirect taxes in India like VAT, Service Tax, and Excise Duty. Introduced on July 1, 2017, GST aims to create a single unified market. In India, GST is primarily divided into four major tax slabs: 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%. Each slab is designed for specific categories of goods and services:

5% Slab

Essential items like sugar, oil, spices, tea, and basic life-saving drugs.

12% Slab

Standard items including butter, cheese, ghee, fruit juices, and mobile phones.

18% Slab

Most services, capital goods, personal care items, and processed foods.

28% Slab

Luxury items and demerit goods like high-end cars, tobacco, and carbonated drinks.

GST in India is dual-structured, meaning it consists of CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST) for intra-state transactions. For example, if an 18% GST rate is applicable, it is split equally into 9% CGST and 9% SGST. For inter-state transactions, a single IGST (Integrated GST) is levied by the Central Government. Businesses with an annual turnover exceeding ₹40 lakhs (for goods) or ₹20 lakhs (for services) are required to register for GST.
